“It has started disagreeing with me.”

“About what?”

“Corners, mostly.”

Ed held the bicycle steady. “Does it refuse to turn?”

“No. It turns. Then it has another opinion.”

“Does the handlebar shake?”

“Shake sounds too definite.”

“Does it wobble?”

“That sounds cheerful.”

“Can you show me?”

“Not here.”

“Why not?”

“It only does it when I am somewhere inconvenient.”

“Traffic?”

“Yes.”

“Excellent.”

“Is it?”

“Traffic is information. What is your name?”

“Marta.”

“Ed. May I ride it?”

“If it lets you.”

Ed wheeled it toward the door.

“Please be careful.”

“I usually am.”

“I meant with yourself.”

He stopped smiling.

“Has it thrown you?”

“Almost.”

“Recently?”

“Tuesday. A bus passed. I thought the wind did something.”

“Did the bicycle move sideways?”

“My stomach moved sideways.”

“Anything else?”

“The front felt late.”

“Late?”

“Like when you ask somebody a question and they pretend they heard.”

Ed returned the bicycle to the counter.

“What happened just before that?”

“I used the brakes.”

“Both?”

“I think so.”

He pointed.

“Oh. Both, then.”

“Let us try something.”

“Will I need the right words?”

“No.”

“The other shop wanted the right words.”

“What did you tell them?”

“That the front felt late.”

“And?”

“They laughed.”

“Hold this brake.”

“The silver thing?”

“Yes. Now push the bicycle forward, then pull it toward you.”

“There. That.”

“The knock?”

“Not a knock.”

“I felt a knock.”

“I felt a tiny betrayal.”

“Again, please.”

She rocked the bicycle.

“There is movement where there should be none.”

“Can you make that less alarming?”

“Two parts need to stay close. They have become distant.”

“That sounds expensive.”

“It depends how long they have ignored each other.”

“Is this how you describe everything?”

“Only before lunch.”

“Will it need a new front?”

“Probably an adjustment.”

“Probably?”

“I must check it first.”

Ed lifted the bicycle into the stand.

“Do you want to wait?”

“Can I?”

“There is a chair.”

“It has a wheel.”

“Three wheels.”

“Does it disagree with people?”

“Only about wages.”

Marta sat.

“Will you tell me what you do?”

“I am checking the bearings.”

“Those are the little balls.”

“Yes.”

“I know one thing.”

“You knew the bicycle felt wrong.”

“That does not count.”

“It brought you here before you fell.”

She watched his hands.

“My daughter says I worry about everything.”

“How old?”

“Fourteen.”

“A qualified expert.”

“She rides behind me. She says I embarrass her.”

“Because you brake?”

“Because I brake, signal, wear this coat, exist near her school.”

“The coat is bright.”

“I need it.”

“For visibility?”

“For warmth.”

Ed turned the fork slowly.

“Did someone work on this recently?”

“My brother changed the handlebar.”

“Higher?”

“I wanted to sit straight.”

“Did the trouble begin afterward?”

“Possibly. He said I would need to adjust to it.”

“People say that about many things.”

“Is it his fault?”

“The adjustment is loose. I cannot tell you who left it loose.”

“He will ask.”

“Tell him the bicycle declined to identify anyone.”

“He hates a mystery.”

“Nothing is damaged.”

“That is unusual.”

“On the bicycle, I mean.”

“Of course.”

Ed worked a wrench.

“What do you call the thing?”

“The headset.”

“Like a telephone.”

“Different job.”

“And you tighten it?”

“I adjust it so the fork turns freely, without that movement.”

“Why not say tighten?”

“Because too tight causes another problem.”

“Everything has such narrow preferences.”

“Especially bicycles.”

“Especially daughters.”

He lowered the bicycle.

“Your turn.”

“To repair it?”

“To try the brake again.”

Marta squeezed the lever and rocked the frame.

“Oh.”

“Better?”

“It arrives with me.”

“That will do.”

“Write that, please.”

“Where?”

“On the receipt.”

“Customer reports bicycle now arrives with customer?”

“Yes.”

“The tax people will enjoy that.”

“How much?”

“Fifteen.”

“For the adjustment?”

“And checking the brakes.”

“What about the translation?”

“Included.”

“I expected something worse.”

“People do.”

She reached for her purse.

“If I ride outside and it still feels wrong?”

“Return.”

“Even if I cannot explain?”

“Especially then.”

“What if nothing is wrong?”

“We can establish that.”

“My brother says confidence matters.”

“It does.”

“So perhaps it was me.”

“Confidence cannot tighten a headset.”

She held the bicycle upright.

“Say that again.”

“Confidence cannot tighten a headset.”

“Put that on the receipt instead.”

Outside, Marta rode to the corner and returned.

“Well?”

“It goes where I go.”

“Good.”

“Without the committee meeting.”

“Good.”

She paid and touched the bell.

“There is one more thing.”

“What does it do?”

She pressed the lever. A ping crossed the shop.

“That.”

“It rings.”

“But listen to it.”

He rang it.

“Excuse me,” she said.

“What?”

“That is what it says. Excuse me. Sorry. Perhaps, if convenient.”

Ed reached beneath the counter.

“How old did you say your daughter was?”

“Fourteen.”

He placed a brass bell between them.

“Try this.”
