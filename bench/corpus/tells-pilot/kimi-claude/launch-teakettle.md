Meet the Marlow, our new stovetop kettle. It is on sale today.

We spent two years on this one. The goal was simple: a kettle that pours well, heats fast, and looks right sitting on your stove every day. No app, no buttons, no base station. Just a kettle that does its job for decades.

The body is spun from a single sheet of stainless steel, with a matte finish in three colors: chalk white, slate, and bare steel. Inside, a layer of aluminum runs up the walls, so heat spreads evenly and water boils faster than in a standard single-wall kettle. On a gas burner, two cups of water reach a rolling boil in about four minutes.

The handle is the part we argued about most. It is made from solid walnut, shaped to sit flat in your palm, and it stays cool enough to grab without a cloth. The lid pulls off with one hand and seats with a soft click. Inside the lid, a small counterweight keeps it from rattling when the boil gets loud.

Pouring is where the Marlow earns its counter space. The spout has a narrow gooseneck profile with a cut lip, so water starts and stops exactly when you tip and straighten. If you brew pour-over coffee, you can hold a thin, steady stream. If you just want tea, the same spout fills a mug without splashing. The kettle holds 1 liter at full capacity and has a marked minimum fill line at 250 ml, so you never run it dry.

It works on every stovetop: gas, electric, ceramic, and induction. The base is flat and wide for good contact on induction rings. Every metal part is dishwasher safe, though we suggest hand washing the walnut handle and oiling it a few times a year.

We back the Marlow with a lifetime warranty on the body and spout. If a weld ever fails, we repair or replace it.

The Marlow costs $95. It is available starting today on our website and at our two retail shops in Portland and Chicago.
