The bell over the door rang. Marisol looked up from the truing stand.

"Morning. What can I do for you?"

"It's my bike. There's something wrong with it."

"Okay. What kind of something?"

The customer set a hand on the saddle of a gray hybrid and rolled it forward. "It's hard to explain."

"Try me. I've heard everything."

"It makes a noise."

"What kind of noise?"

"A weird one."

Marisol waited.

"Like a... a tick. No. A knock. A ticky knock."

"A ticky knock."

"Yeah. But not all the time."

"When does it happen?"

"When I'm riding it."

"That's a start. When you're pedaling? Coasting? Braking?"

The customer frowned at the ceiling. "Pedaling, I think. Mostly."

"Mostly."

"Well, when I pedal hard. Like up the hill on Foster. It goes tick, tick, tick. Or knock. But then sometimes on the flat it does it too, so maybe it's not the pedaling."

"Does it happen when you stand up on the pedals?"

"Stand up?"

"Out of the saddle. Sprint a little."

"I don't sprint."

"Humor me. On the way home, stand up and pedal. If the noise goes away, that tells us something."

"I won't remember by the time I get home."

Marisol pulled a sticky note off the register and wrote on it. She stuck it to the top tube. "There. Homework."

"Can't you just look at it?"

"I can. That's the plan." She lifted the bike into the stand and spun the crank by hand. The chain moved clean over the rings. "When did it start?"

"A while ago."

"Last week? Last year?"

"Somewhere in there. It got worse after I took it to the beach."

"The beach."

"Yeah. It fell over in the sand."

"Okay. That helps." She spun the rear wheel. "Anything else happen to it? Bumps, drops, a roommate backing over it with a car?"

"No. Well. It fell off the rack once."

"Off a car rack?"

"Onto the driveway. But that was before the noise. I think. Actually it might've been after. Does it matter?"

"It all matters. Order matters less than you'd think." She squeezed the rear brake lever, then the front. "Does the noise speed up when you go faster?"

"Maybe? It's hard to hear over the wind and stuff."

"What about when you turn?"

"I don't think so. Wait. Yes. No. Only left turns."

"Only left turns."

"Pretty sure."

"How sure?"

"Forty percent?"

Marisol turned the handlebars and crouched by the front hub. She plucked a spoke. Then another. "Has anyone worked on it recently?"

"My brother-in-law tightened some stuff."

"Which stuff?"

"The stuff that was loose. He said the crank was loose, so he tightened it. He has tools."

"Does the noise happen once per pedal stroke, or randomly?"

"Per pedal... what does that mean?"

"Every time your right foot comes around, tick. Or just whenever it feels like it."

"Oh. Whenever it feels like it. It's got a mind of its own."

"Bikes usually do what physics tells them. People are the unpredictable part." She flipped the bike's kickstand down, then back up. It rattled. She looked at it. She flicked it with one finger. Tick.

"Huh."

"What?"

She grabbed the crank arm and pushed it side to side. A faint click, barely there. "Bottom bracket's got some play. And your kickstand bolt is loose enough to dance."

"Is the dancing the ticky knock?"

"The kickstand is one noise. The bottom bracket is another. You might have a whole percussion section in here."

"That's bad?"

"That's fixable. Sand doesn't help. Falls don't help. Your brother-in-law's enthusiasm didn't help."

"He meant well."

"They always do." She pulled the chain off the ring and flexed it. Two links stuck at an angle like a bent finger. "And here. Stiff links. This is your tick, most likely. The knock is the bottom bracket. The kickstand is the free bonus track."

"So you found it? Without me even describing it right?"

"You described it fine. Tick versus knock, worse uphill, maybe left turns. That's more than I usually get. Last week a guy told me his bike felt, quote, emotionally off."

"Emotionally off?"

"He needed air in his tires."

The customer laughed. "So what's the damage?"

"Chain clean and lube, tighten the bottom bracket, kickstand bolt. Forty minutes. Call it sixty bucks. If the bracket's shot, add thirty and a day."

"Do it." The customer peeled the sticky note off the top tube. "Guess I don't need this."

"Keep it. Stand up on the hill anyway. If it still ticks after all this, I want to know."

"And if it ticks when I stand?"

"Then it's your knees, and I can't help you."

Marisol wheeled the bike toward the back. "Coffee shop next door. Come back in an hour."

"How will I know it's fixed?"

"You'll know. It'll be the quietest ride of your life."

"It was never that loud."

"It never is," Marisol said. "Until it is."
